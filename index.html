01

LGTO · ³He–⁴He DILUTION REFRIGERATION · THERMODYNAMICS

Probing a frustrated magnet
down to 100 mK.

A central part of my work on Li₃Gd₃Te₂O₁₂ is low-temperature heat-capacity measurement using a ³He–⁴He dilution refrigerator at UGC-DAE CSR, Indore. These measurements reveal the magnetic thermodynamics underlying its sub-kelvin cooling performance.

Extending heat capacity down to 100 mK allows us to resolve antiferromagnetic ordering near 250 mK and investigate how applied magnetic fields reshape the low-temperature magnetic state. I connect this thermodynamic picture with field-induced critical behaviour, entropy accumulation, and direct ADR measurements.

Dilution-refrigerator measurements & ADR results

Magnetic thermodynamics in the millikelvin regime

My experimental experience includes sub-kelvin heat-capacity measurements and assistance with helium-3 filling. Temperature- and field-dependent thermodynamic data, together with magnetic Grüneisen analysis and field–temperature phase diagrams, probe low-temperature ordering and field-induced critical behaviour in LGTO.

Connecting thermodynamics to refrigeration

Direct adiabatic demagnetization measurements in a separate custom ADR setup reached 165 mK. The cooling response demonstrates how magnetic entropy retained at low temperature can be used for sub-kelvin refrigeration. The dilution refrigerator provides the thermodynamic characterization needed to understand that performance.

Research supervised by Dr. Deepshikha Jaiswal-Nagar; dilution-refrigerator measurements at UGC-DAE CSR, Indore.

02

LNTO · MARI / ISIS · INELASTIC NEUTRON SCATTERING

Resolving crystal-field excitations.
Understanding Jeff = ½.

The centrepiece of my LNTO research is the analysis of inelastic neutron scattering (INS) data measured on the MARI time-of-flight spectrometer at the ISIS Neutron and Muon Source, UK. I use these spectra to investigate the Nd³⁺ crystal-field excitations and the effective Jeff = ½ ground state in Li₃Nd₃Te₂O₁₂.

My analysis combines spectral fitting with PyCrystalField modelling to determine the crystal-field level scheme and relate it to magnetization, susceptibility, and heat-capacity measurements.

For Nd³⁺, the J = 9/2 ground multiplet contains ten states. Crystal electric fields split this manifold into five Kramers doublets. When the excited doublets are thermally inaccessible, the lowest doublet provides an effective two-state, or pseudospin-½, degree of freedom.

INS analysis & supporting thermodynamic evidence

MARI spectra and crystal-field modelling

My work focuses on analysing the magnetic excitation spectrum measured at MARI, fitting the observed spectral features, and using PyCrystalField to constrain the crystal-field description of Nd³⁺. This connects the excitation energies with the ground-state doublet and the calculated magnetic response.

Structure and susceptibility

Powder XRD and Rietveld refinement support a single-phase cubic garnet structure, with Nd³⁺ moments forming a hyperkagome network. High- and low-temperature Curie–Weiss fits reveal a reduced effective moment as excited crystal-field states become depopulated. A two-level fit to susceptibility gives a crystal-field gap estimate of approximately 165 K.

Magnetization and heat capacity

The 2 K magnetization is described by an effective J = ½ Brillouin fit, with g ≈ 2.17. A Debye–Einstein model estimates the phonon background in the heat capacity. The field-induced contribution develops a broad Schottky peak that shifts to higher temperature with increasing field.

Two-level Schottky fits yield an approximately field-linear energy gap, consistent with Zeeman splitting of the ground doublet. The associated effective g ≈ 1.98 provides complementary thermodynamic support for the pseudospin-½ interpretation.

ADR and low-energy magnetism

ADR experiments using a custom puck and ruthenium-oxide thermometry probe the cooling response below 1 K. These measurements connect the accessible magnetic entropy with refrigeration performance; the 282 mK minimum is a cooling result, rather than a direct determination of an ordering temperature.

Connecting thermodynamics with spectroscopy

Building on the thermodynamic measurements, my subsequent work includes analysis of MARI time-of-flight inelastic neutron scattering data from ISIS. Spectral fitting and PyCrystalField modelling connect the observed excitations with the crystal-field level scheme and magnetic response.

Together, these measurements support a low-temperature Kramers-doublet description. They motivate further investigation of quantum magnetism in LNTO, without by themselves establishing a quantum spin liquid.

The Phase 2 thesis documents the thermodynamic foundation of this work; the research has since progressed to INS data analysis and CEF modelling.

HIGH-ENTROPY ALLOY DESIGN · ROOM-TEMPERATURE COOLING

High-entropy alloys for
room-temperature cooling.

The room-temperature branch of my Phase 1 research explores high-entropy alloy candidates for magnetic cooling. It complements the same report’s experimental study of LGTO for sub-kelvin refrigeration. Chemical substitution in Mn-based intermetallics is used to investigate how magnetic and structural transitions can be brought toward room temperature.

The focus is the MnTX family, with transition-metal and Si/Ge-based substitutions. I connect composition screening with electronic-structure calculations, magnetic modelling, and laboratory preparation using arc melting and quartz-tube sealing.

Composition study & research direction

A screened Mn–Fe–Ni–Si–Ge composition

The research report evaluates Fe₀.₅Mn₀.₅NiSi₀.₉₅Ge₀.₀₅ through ideal-mixing entropy, mixing enthalpy, and atomic-radius mismatch. It reports ΔSmix ≈ 1.39R and a size-mismatch parameter of approximately 6.01%.

The entropy estimate lies below the report’s 1.5R high-entropy criterion, while the mixing-enthalpy estimate also calls for further assessment. This makes the composition a candidate for continued study, rather than proof of a stable, single-phase high-entropy alloy.

Tuning the magnetocaloric response

The motivation is to tune magnetic and structural transitions through substitution in MnNiSi-derived systems, including Fe/Mn mixing and Al/Ge substitution. Coupled magnetic and structural changes provide a route to investigating enhanced magnetocaloric response near ambient temperature.

This work combines composition screening with subsequent SPR-KKR/Monte Carlo calculations and hands-on alloy synthesis. The goal is to identify viable magnetocaloric candidates; room-temperature refrigeration performance remains to be established.

Based on “Adiabatic demagnetization as a pathway toward cryogenic and ambient magnetic refrigeration” and subsequent work described in my CV.
