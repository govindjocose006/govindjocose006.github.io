01

LGTO · ³He–⁴He DILUTION REFRIGERATION · MAGNETIC COOLING

Probing magnetic criticality.
Cooling below the ordering temperature.

My work on Li₃Gd₃Te₂O₁₂ (LGTO) connects millikelvin magnetic thermodynamics with direct adiabatic demagnetization refrigeration (ADR). Heat-capacity measurements using a ³He–⁴He dilution refrigerator at UGC-DAE CSR, Indore, probe ordering near 250 mK and the effect of magnetic field on this frustrated garnet.

Alongside these measurements, a separate PPMS-integrated ADR setup reached 165 mK. Comparing the cooling response, field-dependent heat capacity and magnetic Grüneisen analysis helps us investigate how entropy accumulation near critical fields can enhance magnetic cooling.

Scientific question
Can field-induced quantum criticality and the associated entropy accumulation enhance adiabatic demagnetization cooling in a frustrated magnet?
My contribution
Prepared the polycrystalline sample; submitted it for powder XRD and checked phase purity using FullProf/Rietveld refinement; made sample contacts; performed PPMS VSM, heat-capacity and ADR measurements; analysed the data and assisted in writing the manuscript.
Dilution-refrigerator work
Participated in zero-field and field-dependent heat-capacity measurements down to 100 mK at UGC-DAE CSR, Indore, using a ³He–⁴He dilution refrigerator.
Key outcomes
ADR reached 165 mK at 2.28 T, below the ordering temperature of approximately 250 mK. The reported setup maintained temperatures below 1 K for more than 12 hours, starting from 1.8 K and 7 T.

My measurements, analysis & what I learned

From sample preparation to thermodynamics

I prepared the polycrystalline material by solid-state synthesis and assessed the powder XRD pattern using Rietveld refinement. I made sample contacts and performed PPMS magnetization, heat-capacity and ADR measurements. At UGC-DAE CSR, I participated in the dilution-refrigerator measurements that extended heat-capacity characterization to the millikelvin regime.

Reading the low-temperature magnetic state

The zero-field heat capacity resolves magnetic ordering near 250 mK. Field-dependent magnetic heat capacity at 100 mK shows anomalies near 1 T, 1.45 T and 2.28 T. The study uses these features, together with the cooling response, to investigate field-induced phases and the approach to a polarized state.

Connecting critical behaviour with cooling

The magnetic Grüneisen parameter, calculated from the quasi-adiabatic cooling response, changes sign near the fields associated with enhanced cooling. Together with heat-capacity anomalies, this supports entropy accumulation and field-induced critical behaviour. The phase assignments shown in the figure are the interpretation proposed in the study.

Demonstrating refrigeration directly

The ADR experiment uses a custom setup integrated with the PPMS. It is separate from the dilution refrigerator used for heat capacity. Starting from approximately 1.8 K and 7 T, demagnetization reaches a minimum of 165 mK at 2.28 T; the supplied trace shows approximately 220 mK at zero field. The manuscript reports more than 3 hours below 0.4 K and more than 12 hours below 1 K in the measured setup.

What I learned

This project taught me to connect material preparation and structural characterization with magnetic measurements, millikelvin thermodynamics and refrigeration performance. Analysing heat capacity alongside the cooling trace helps distinguish magnetic features from the influence of heat leaks and thermal contact. Cooling performance depends on both the material’s entropy landscape and the experimental conditions.

Research supervised by Dr. Deepshikha Jaiswal-Nagar; dilution-refrigerator measurements at UGC-DAE CSR, Indore. I analysed the data and assisted in writing the manuscript, which has been accepted for publication.

02

LNTO · MARI / ISIS · INELASTIC NEUTRON SCATTERING

Resolving crystal-field excitations.
Exploring effective spin-½ magnetism.

I investigate how the Nd³⁺ crystal-field levels shape low-temperature magnetism in Li₃Nd₃Te₂O₁₂ (LNTO), a rare-earth garnet with a hyperkagome network of magnetic ions. A central part of this work is my analysis of inelastic neutron scattering (INS) spectra collected on the MARI time-of-flight spectrometer at the ISIS Neutron and Muon Source, UK.

I combine INS analysis, spectral fitting and PyCrystalField calculations with structural characterization, magnetization, heat capacity and direct adiabatic demagnetization measurements.

Scientific question
Does the Nd³⁺ crystal-field ground doublet provide an effective spin-½ degree of freedom, and how does this relate to LNTO’s magnetic and sub-kelvin cooling response?
My contribution
Polycrystalline sample preparation and characterization; Rietveld refinement and fitting; sample contacts; PPMS VSM, heat-capacity and ADR measurements; INS data analysis, spectral fitting and PyCrystalField calculations.
Neutron experiment
I prepared the sample and shipped it to ISIS via FedEx for measurements at MARI. My neutron-scattering contribution is sample preparation and shipment, data analysis and modelling.
What the evidence supports
Bulk measurements and candidate CEF excitations support a low-temperature Kramers-doublet description. ADR reached 282 mK at 1.189 T and 762 mK at zero field.

My measurements, analysis & what I learned

From synthesis to bulk measurements

I prepared the polycrystalline material, carried out characterization and Rietveld refinement, made sample contacts, and performed the PPMS magnetization, heat-capacity and ADR measurements. Powder diffraction and refinement support the cubic garnet structure, with Nd³⁺ ions forming a network of corner-sharing triangles.

From neutron spectra to a CEF description

I analysed the MARI INS data, fitted spectral features and performed PyCrystalField calculations to investigate the excitation energies and magnetic response. Comparing spectra at 5, 50 and 200 K helps test candidate CEF assignments against the redistribution of intensity as excited levels become thermally populated.

Why an effective spin-½ description?

The Nd³⁺ J = 9/2 manifold can split into five Kramers doublets. At temperatures sufficiently below the first excited doublet, the ground doublet can be represented by an effective pseudospin-½. This description allows an anisotropic magnetic response; it does not require the atomic angular momentum to become J = ½.

Connecting magnetism and thermodynamics

The reduced low-temperature effective moment, nonlinear 2 K magnetization and field-dependent heat-capacity peaks support a doublet-based interpretation. The broad heat-capacity features shift with field, consistent with Zeeman splitting. Susceptibility shows no clear ordering anomaly down to its measurement limit of 1.8 K.

Sub-kelvin magnetic refrigeration

Direct ADR measurements using a custom puck and ruthenium-oxide thermometry reached 282 mK at 1.189 T and 762 mK at zero field. These values characterize the cooling response; determining the collective magnetic state at these temperatures requires further low-temperature measurements.

What I learned

Combining spectroscopy with bulk measurements is essential: a model that describes susceptibility alone must also be tested against INS energies, transition intensities and thermodynamics. This project developed my experience across sample preparation, cryogenic measurements, neutron-data analysis and CEF modelling.

The available evidence supports a low-temperature Kramers-doublet description. The detailed CEF parameters, magnetic anisotropy and collective sub-kelvin ground state remain questions for further investigation.

Research supervised by Dr. Deepshikha Jaiswal-Nagar. The Phase 2 thesis documents the bulk measurements; the INS maps show the subsequent MARI / ISIS investigation. View the thesis.

HIGH-ENTROPY ALLOY DESIGN · ROOM-TEMPERATURE COOLING

High-entropy alloys for
room-temperature cooling.

The room-temperature branch of my Phase 1 research explores high-entropy alloy candidates for magnetic cooling. It complements the same report’s experimental study of LGTO for sub-kelvin refrigeration. Chemical substitution in Mn-based intermetallics is used to investigate how magnetic and structural transitions can be brought toward room temperature.

The focus is the MnTX family, with transition-metal and Si/Ge-based substitutions. I connect composition screening with electronic-structure calculations, magnetic modelling, and laboratory preparation using arc melting and quartz-tube sealing.

Research question
Can composition tuning bring magnetic and structural transitions toward room temperature?
My contribution
Composition screening, SPR-KKR and exchange-interaction calculations, Monte Carlo modelling, and laboratory preparation using arc melting and tube sealing.
Key outcome
A route for evaluating Mn-based magnetic-cooling candidates. The screened composition is a candidate for further study; a working room-temperature refrigerant has not been demonstrated in the linked report.

Composition study & research direction

A screened Mn–Fe–Ni–Si–Ge composition

The research report evaluates Fe₀.₅Mn₀.₅NiSi₀.₉₅Ge₀.₀₅ through ideal-mixing entropy, mixing enthalpy, and atomic-radius mismatch. It reports ΔSmix ≈ 1.39R and a size-mismatch parameter of approximately 6.01%.

The entropy estimate lies below the report’s 1.5R high-entropy criterion, while the mixing-enthalpy estimate also calls for further assessment. This makes the composition a candidate for continued study, rather than proof of a stable, single-phase high-entropy alloy.

Tuning the magnetocaloric response

The motivation is to tune magnetic and structural transitions through substitution in MnNiSi-derived systems, including Fe/Mn mixing and Al/Ge substitution. Coupled magnetic and structural changes provide a route to investigating enhanced magnetocaloric response near ambient temperature.

This work combines composition screening with subsequent SPR-KKR/Monte Carlo calculations and hands-on alloy synthesis. The goal is to identify viable magnetocaloric candidates; room-temperature refrigeration performance remains to be established.

Based on “Adiabatic demagnetization as a pathway toward cryogenic and ambient magnetic refrigeration” and subsequent work described in my CV.
